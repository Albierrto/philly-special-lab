How this lineup is picked
  • Every hitter on your roster gets an expected points number for the day: his 2026 points per plate appearance, times how many trips he usually gets, then nudged up or down for the opposing starter (how good he is, and lefty/righty splits), the ballpark, the weather at first pitch, playing at home, and his last 30 days.
  • The ten lineup spots (C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, three OF, two UT) are filled with the highest expected points, scarce positions first. Once clubs post lineups, anyone left out of his club's lineup is moved to the bench automatically.
  • The chips under each name say why: green helps, red hurts, and the size is the percent change to his expected points.

Trade analyzer

Put players and draft picks on each side and see what the trade does to both teams, in this league's points. Built for a keeper league: only the five hitters and two pitchers a team keeps carry any value into next year, so a player is worth what he adds to that team's best keeper group, not a number in a vacuum.

How a trade is scored
  • Value next year = a player's projected 2027 points minus what a replacement-level player would give you (the best bat or arm sitting on waivers). We call that points above replacement.
  • Keeper value looks past 2027 too: next year counts in full, 2028 at 60% and 2029 at 35%, with each player aged along the league's own aging curve. A 24-year-old and a 33-year-old with the same 2027 projection are not the same asset.
  • Team effect: we rebuild each team's best keeper group (5 hitters + 2 pitchers) before and after the trade. A sixth good hitter is only worth what he adds over the fifth one he pushes out; a player who would not be kept is worth nothing for next year because he goes back into the draft.
  • Draft picks are valued from two halves: what the 2027 board says is left at that pick after every team's likely keepers are removed, and what picks at that slot have actually returned in this league's 2024–2026 drafts. The draft is fixed order (no snake), so a team's round-2 pick is the same slot as its round-1 pick, 12 picks later. Slots come from the final regular-season standings: 9th–12th place pick 1–4 in that order, then 8th down to 1st.
  • Right now mode also adds each hitter's and pitcher's expected points for the rest of this week, from the Streamers model.

How the numbers are made

Each score in plain words first, then the math behind it if you want it. Every check below was run on past seasons the model had not seen.

League points (verified against Fantrax)

Everything on this site is scored the way this league scores. Hits, homers, runs, RBI, walks and steals earn points for hitters; innings, strikeouts and quality starts earn points for pitchers, while earned runs and blown saves cost points.

Deserved points

What a hitter should have scored based on how hard and where he hit the ball, using Statcast's expected stats instead of his actual hits. Walks, hit-by-pitches and steals are skills, so they stay as they were. Luck = actual points − deserved points: a big positive number means the hits fell in and he is likely to come back down.

Show the math
xH = xBA × AB; xXBH = xISO × AB / (r2B + 2·r3B + 3·rHR); x1B = xH − xXBH x2B = xXBH·r2B; x3B = xXBH·r3B; xHR = xXBH·rHR (his own doubles/triples/homers mix, regressed to league over 40 XBH) xR/PA, xRBI/PA = ridge(xOBP, xISO, xBA, SB/PA, xHR/PA, sprint) deserved points = Σ weight(event) × x(event); luck = points − deserved points Check: across 987 consecutive 300-PA seasons, deserved points per PA predicted next year's points per PA with r = 0.52, actual points per PA only 0.47.

Points above replacement

How many points a player gives you over the best free agent you could plug into his spot. Catchers are compared with catchers; everyone else with the pool as a whole. It is the fairest way to compare a shortstop with a first baseman.

Show the math
fill C, SS, 2B, 3B, 1B, OF for every team with the best available hitters, then the UT slots replacement(pos) = points of the best hitter still unrostered at that position (+2 bench bats per team) points above replacement = points − min(replacement at his positions, replacement at UT)

Beat his draft slot by

How many more (or fewer) points a hitter produced than the typical hitter drafted where he was drafted, using that year's preseason ADP. "Beat" means +75 points, 25 spots better than drafted, and a top-150 finish.

Show the math
expected(rank) = isotonic( rolling median of points by preseason ADP hitter rank ), per season beat slot by = points − expected(ADP rank); rank gain = ADP rank − final rank beat: ≥ +75 pts, ≥ +25 ranks, top-150 finish big beat: ≥ +150, ≥ +60, top-75

Aging curve

How much a hitter's points per plate appearance typically change from one age to the next, measured from every hitter in 2021–2026 with a full season both years. This league rewards singles and steals, which fade early, so the curve turns down harder and sooner than a home-run league's would. The projection and the trade analyzer both age players along this curve.

Projected 2027 points

A player's expected points next year: his rate times the plate appearances he can be expected to get. The rate is three parts a skills model (deserved points, contact quality, discipline, speed; it knows that very productive seasons usually give some back) and one part his own last three seasons, then moved a step along the aging curve. Playing time comes from his pace while he was actually on the roster, so a late call-up or an injury-shortened year does not read as a part-time player, and it is trimmed for injury history (this year's IL days in full, last year's 60%, two years ago 30%) and age.

Show the math
rate = 0.75 × model(Statcast skills, deserved points, career) + 0.25 × track + ½ × aging step(age) track = PA-weighted pts/PA over the last three seasons with 150+ PA, weights 1.0 / 0.6 / 0.3 (this year / last / two ago) pace = PA per 162 team games while on the active roster (IL stints and days before the call-up excluded) weighted IL days = this year + 0.6 × last year + 0.3 × two years ago projected PA = (0.6 × 2026 pace + 0.4 × mean pace 2024-26, seasons with 150+ PA) × (1 − 0.0008 × weighted IL days, cap 250) × (0.95 if age ≥ 33), clipped 200-700 projected points = rate × projected PA Check (leave-one-season-out, 300+ PA): the blend predicts next year's pts/PA with r = 0.52 vs 0.49 for the model alone and 0.46 for carrying this year over.

Breakout %

The model's chance that next season is a new career level for a hitter: points per plate appearance at least 0.08 above his previous best, with 350+ plate appearances and a top-90 finish. It looks at his Statcast skills, the gap between deserved and actual points, how far he sits below his own best, his age, playing time and injury history, and how his strikeouts, barrels and bat speed changed this year. A veteran who already had his best years cannot score high, by design.

Show the math
breakout(t+1) := pts/PA(t+1) ≥ career-best(200+ PA seasons through t) + 0.08 AND PA(t+1) ≥ 350 AND final rank(t+1) ≤ 90 Breakout % = 100 × P(breakout | skills, deserved-points gap, career best, gap to best, seasons, age, PA, IL history, yoy K%/barrel/bat-speed change) gradient-boosted classifier trained on 2021→22 … 2025→26, checked leave-one-season-out

Breakout %, leave-one-season-out

AUC is how well the model separates breakouts from the rest (0.5 = coin flip, 1 = perfect). Top-40 hit = share of its 40 highest scores who really broke out, against the base rate for everyone.

Points projection, leave-one-season-out

r = correlation with what actually happened the next year; higher is better. "Carry-over" is the naive forecast that next year equals this year.

Using 2025 data only, the 25 highest Breakout % hitters produced a new-level 2026 at a 16% clip against a 3.3% base rate; Caglianone, Otto Lopez, Crow-Armstrong and Meidroth were all inside that top 25. Trout's 2027 Breakout % is 1.9.

Keeper value

How many more points a player projects for 2027 than the best player you could draft instead of keeping him: the best hitter (or pitcher) left on the board once every team has removed its likely keepers, at a first-round pick. Positive means keeping him beats drafting. Lock is 120+ for hitters and 80+ for pitchers, clear keep 40+ / 25+, marginal above zero, let go below. "2nd" is the same idea measured against a second-round pick.

Show the math
keeper value = projected points − projected points of the player ranked (keepers league-wide + 12) keeper value 2nd = projected points − projected points of the player ranked (keepers league-wide + 24) hitters and pitchers each have their own board; a two-way player kept as a pitcher carries pitching + hitting projection

Trade value and draft picks

A trade is scored by rebuilding each team's best keeper group (5 hitters + 2 pitchers) before and after. A player's worth to a team is what he adds to that group in points above replacement, counting 2027 in full, 2028 at 60% and 2029 at 35%, aged along the curves above. A draft pick is worth half of what the 2027 board says is left at that pick and half of what picks at that slot have actually returned in this league since 2024 (a bust counts as zero: you drop him and stream the spot).

Show the math
points above replacement (PAR) = projected points − projected points of the (12 × 13)th hitter or (12 × 7)th starting pitcher on the board value(player, year) = PAR_2027 + 0.6 × max(0, PAR_2028) + 0.35 × max(0, PAR_2029), each year aged by the hitter or pitcher aging curve team keeper value = Σ value over its best 5 hitters + best 2 pitchers (others return to the draft and count 0) trade effect on a team = keeper value after − before + Σ picks received − Σ picks given (+ this week's expected points in "right now" mode) pick value(k) = 0.5 × PAR of the k-th best player left after likely keepers + 0.5 × smoothed realized PAR of pick k in the 2024-26 drafts pick k for a team = (round − 1) × 12 + its draft slot (fixed order, no snake); a pick a year further out is worth 80%

League fit

How much better or worse a hitter looks in this league's scoring than in a standard points league or a 5×5 roto league, in percentile points. Speed-and-contact hitters fit here; three-true-outcome sluggers fit less well.

Show the math
fit vs points = percentile(pts/PA, this scoring) − percentile(pts/PA, ESPN-style points with K = −1) fit vs roto = percentile(pts/PA, this scoring) − percentile(5x5 roto z-score)

Prospect score

A 0–100 grade for how close a minor-league hitter is to helping: pedigree (MLB Pipeline rank) is 35 of the points, being young for his level is 25, his minor-league bat (power, on-base, walks vs strikeouts, steals, compared with hitters at the same level) is 25, and the quality of any big-league sample (contact quality, bat speed) is 15.

Show the math
prospect score = 35·pedigree + 25·level_age + 25·milb_bat + 15·mlb_sample pedigree = 1 − (Pipeline rank − 1)/100 (0.15 unranked); level_age = clip(0.5 + (avg age at level − age)·0.15, 0, 1) × level weight (AAA 1, AA .85, A+ .6, A .4) milb_bat = 0.5 + 0.2·z( ISO, OBP, BB−K, ½·SB/600 ) within level; mlb_sample = mean percentile of xwOBA, EV50, bat speed

Each minor-league line on a prospect card is colored by its percentile against every hitter with 100+ PA at the same level in the same season. The Statcast block comes from Savant's minor-league search (Triple-A parks carry Hawk-Eye): exit velocity, barrels, whiffs and chases, with percentiles inside that season's prospect cohort.

Pitching: Stuff+, Location+, Pitching+

Three grades on a 100-is-average scale, built the way FanGraphs' and PitcherList's versions are framed. Stuff+ grades the pitches themselves (whiffs, in-zone misses, put-away rate, velocity, how unusual the movement is). Location+ grades command (edges, first strikes, chases, walks, meatballs). Pitching+ is the blend that best predicts next year's points per start. The real models use pitch-by-pitch tracking that is not public; these are season-level stand-ins from Savant's leaderboards, and the check below is what they are worth in this league's scoring.

Show the math
Stuff+ = 100 + 10·z[ z(whiff%) + z(in-zone swing-miss%) + z(arsenal put-away%) + z(fastball velo) + ½·z(movement uniqueness) + z(arsenal RV/100 vs hitters) ] / 5.5 Location+ = 100 + 10·z[ z(edge%) + z(first-strike%) + ½·z(zone%) − z(meatball%) − z(BB%) + z(chase%) ] / 5.5 Pitching+ = 100 + 10·z[ ridge(Stuff+, Location+, xERA, K−BB%) → next-year points per start ] arsenal: pitch types with 10+% usage; put-away = K / 2-strike pitches; z-scores among starters with 50+ IP in the same season

Pitcher breakout % and projection

Same idea as the hitter version: the chance a starter's next season is a new career level in points per start, and a separate projection of points per start times projected starts (trimmed for injury history and age).

Streamers

A hitter-day is worth his points per plate appearance × his usual plate appearances, nudged for the opposing starter (his quality this year, plus the hitter's lefty/righty split), the park (Savant's three-year run factors by batter side), the weather at first pitch (temperature and the wind blowing out to center, from the park's orientation), home field and his last 30 days. A pitcher's start is worth his points per start (starts only, from game logs), nudged for the opposing lineup's quality and strikeouts against his hand, the park and the weather. Rotations are projected from each club's recent starters until the probable is posted; the website re-reads the posted probables, lineups, scores and forecasts every ten minutes.

Show the math
hitter exp pts = base pts/PA × PA/G × f_SP^0.6 × f_platoon × f_park × f_weather × f_home × f_form f_SP = clip(SP xwOBA / league xwOBA, 0.75, 1.3), regressed to league over 300 batters faced; f_platoon from his wOBA vs L / vs R (regressed); f_park = Savant runs factor by batter side / 100 f_weather = clip(1 + 0.003 × (temp − 70), 0.94, 1.06) × clip(1 + 0.004 × wind-out mph, 0.92, 1.08), 1.0 under a roof; f_form from 30-day wOBA (regressed) pitcher exp pts = base pts/GS × f_opp × f_park^0.5 × f_weather^0.5 × f_home + K bonus from the opponent's K% vs that hand f_opp = 1 − 3.5·(opponent wOBA vs his hand − .312), capped at ±30%; K bonus = 0.85 · his K per start · (opponent K% / .222 − 1) those two strengths were checked against the same 3,072 starts: a .020 swing in the opponent's wOBA is worth 0.71 points, three points of opponent K% 0.56, and home 0.60 (most of which the park factor already carries) base pts/GS = 0.27 + 0.110·Pitching+ + 1.203·IP-mix + 0.039·ΔCSW + 0.808·Δvelo − 26.70·xwOBA against IP-mix = half his innings per start this season, half his last three starts (shrunk toward 5.2 IP on a thin sample) ΔCSW, Δvelo = called-strikes-plus-whiffs per pitch, and fastball velocity, over the last 30 days against his own season — both measured over the games he STARTED, because a reliever's one max-effort inning is not his starting velocity. Filtering the trend to starts is worth a lot: velocity carries 0.84 points a start per mph that way, against 0.15 when relief outings are mixed in. fitted on 3,072 real 2026 starts, each predicted from what was known before it, five-fold by pitcher a starter with no Savant line falls back to his own starts-only rate shrunk toward what his Pitching+ implies Which of the metrics analysts name actually predict THIS league's points, one at a time, against the next start: Pitching+ .332 xwOBA against .327 Stuff+ .320 strikeouts per start .291 his own points per start .294 K−BB% .260 K% .247 IP per start .229 SwStr% .227 CSW% .212 whiff% .205 ERA .197 BB% .105 Two of those readings are specific to this league. Strikeouts per START beat K% (.291 vs .247), because a strikeout is worth a point here rather than propping up a rate. And BB% barely registers, because there is no walk penalty at all — a walk only costs through the baserunner and the shorter outing. K−BB% is the analysts' simple workhorse and it does beat K% here, but contact quality beats both: xwOBA against earned a place in the formula, while strikeouts per start, quality-start rate and K−BB% all took a zero weight once Pitching+ and innings were already in. End to end, against the same 3,072 starts with the matchup multipliers applied: r 0.380, MAE 4.49, against r 0.375 and 4.50 for the version before contact quality went in, and r 0.315 / 4.67 for the original season-rate blend. Two things were tried and left out because they made it worse. Recent RESULTS: predicting the next start from the last three starts alone scores r 0.241, worse than the season-to-date rate's 0.288, and a regression given both puts almost no weight on the recent one. Starts-only SKILL LEVELS: rebuilding the level term from his CSW, K−BB% and velocity over his starts alone scores r 0.303 against 0.364 for season-long Pitching+ across every appearance, and when both are offered the fit drops the starts-only version entirely. Relief innings add sample to a pitcher's stuff rather than noise — it is the trend, not the level, that has to be filtered to starts. A pitcher whose last outing came out of the bullpen, or who has not started in twelve days, is left out of the projected rotations entirely — a posted probable still overrides it.
