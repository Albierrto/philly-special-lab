How this lineup is picked
  • Every hitter on your roster gets an expected points number for the day: his 2026 points per plate appearance, times how many trips he usually gets, then nudged up or down for the opposing starter (how good he is, and lefty/righty splits), the ballpark, the weather at first pitch, playing at home, and his last 30 days.
  • The ten lineup spots (C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, three OF, two UT) are filled with the highest expected points, scarce positions first. Once clubs post lineups, anyone left out of his club's lineup is moved to the bench automatically.
  • The chips under each name say why: green helps, red hurts, and the size is the percent change to his expected points.

Trades

Start from a player in the deal desk, or put players, minor leaguers and draft picks on each side of the analyzer and see what the trade does to every team in it (two or three), in this league's points. Built for a keeper league: only the five hitters and two pitchers a team keeps carry any value into next year, so a player is worth what he adds to that team's best keeper group, not a number in a vacuum.

How a trade is scored
  • Value next year = what a player beats a free agent by on the days he plays: projected 2027 points minus about 3.5 a game for a hitter (a little less at catcher) and 7.2 a start for a pitcher. Those rates were measured by replaying 2026 in this league's format. We call that points above replacement.
  • Multi-year value looks past 2027 too, as an option: in 2028 and 2029 a player only adds what he is expected to beat the league's keeper line by, because that is the only way he gets kept again, discounted 0.8 a year and aged along the league's own curve. A 24-year-old star and a 33-year-old with the same 2027 projection are not the same asset; a player who will not be keeper-worthy next year carries almost nothing past 2027.
  • Team effect: we rebuild each team's best keeper group (5 hitters + 2 pitchers) before and after the trade. A sixth good hitter is only worth what he adds over the fifth one he pushes out; a player who would not be kept is worth nothing for next year because he goes back into the draft.
  • Draft picks are valued from two halves: what the 2027 board says is left at that pick after every team's likely keepers are removed, and what picks at that slot have actually returned in this league's 2024–2026 drafts. The draft is fixed order (no snake), so a team's round-2 pick is the same slot as its round-1 pick, 12 picks later. Slots come from the final regular-season standings: 9th–12th place pick 1–4 in that order, then 8th down to 1st.
  • Right now mode also adds each hitter's and pitcher's expected points for the rest of this week, from the Streamers model.
  • The deal desk searches instead of guessing. For a player you want, it tries every package from your roster of one or two players, one or two picks, a player and a pick, or two players and a pick, and keeps the ones his team would come out ahead on; when you would be overpaying it also tries them handing a pick back. For a player you are selling, it does the same from every other roster, or from the one team you pick. A player thrown in has to make the keeper group he is joining, or he is not really part of the price. Fair to both ranks by the smaller of the two gains, so the top deal is the one most likely to be accepted; Best for me ranks by your gain alone, as long as they still gain something. When no deal helps both sides, the desk shows the price instead: the cheapest packages they would take, with what that costs you.
  • To even it, under the verdict, is the single pick or player from the side that is ahead that brings the trade closest to level.
  • Minor leaguers can be traded too. A team keeps up to two of them on top of its 5 hitters and 2 pitchers, as long as they have never played in the majors (the green flag on Fantrax); a slot left empty is filled in the minor-league draft. So a minor leaguer is worth what he beats the prospect that slot would hold anyway. His value is on the same three-year scale as everyone else. For a ranked prospect it comes from his MLB Pipeline rank, measured on what ranked prospects who had not yet played actually returned over their next three seasons (2021-24 lists): the top three averaged about 200, ranks 4-10 about 80, 11-25 about 30, and 26-100 about 10-15. The rank carries almost all of it; his stat line and level only decide when the value arrives. An unranked prospect is valued from minor leaguers like him (level, age for it, bat or arm) and what they returned.
  • Draft picks are the ones each team actually holds on Fantrax, including traded ones. A traded pick keeps its original team's slot, and a team holding more picks than it has roster spots (15, after 7 keepers) only gets value from the extra ones by what they beat.
  • Nothing lopsided gets suggested. A trade can leave both keeper groups better off and still be one no commissioner would allow (Kurtz for a fourth-rounder helps Basketball only because Sal Stewart would take his spot). So every suggested deal also has to be close on what changes hands: each asset at the most any roster in the league would gain from it, with the best piece counting for more than the pieces around it, and each side getting at least 80% of what it gives. The analyzer flags any trade that fails that test.
  • Three-team deals are rotations: you send a player to one team, it sends one to a third, and the third sends one to you. Each team is scored on what it sends and receives, exactly as in a two-team deal, and a rotation is listed only when all three gain and a straight swap with the third team would not work on its own. In the analyzer, Add a third team lets you build any three-way shape and choose where each player goes.

Breakout candidates for

A breakout is new territory for that hitter: a career-best points-per-PA rate, at least 0.08 over his previous best, across 350+ plate appearances and a top-90 finish. The percentage is the chance of that. Three numbers sit under each man: what he did this year, the model's typical 2027 (the middle of what next year could be), and his breakout season (what the year looks like if he does it). A man can project for fewer points than he scored and still lead this list: his typical year is a small step back, his upside is a new level. Under each card: the reasons in the model's own arithmetic, his Statcast line with where each number sits among this year's hitters, and the player-seasons that looked most like him with what they did next.

Is it just handing young regulars a number?

Partly, and on purpose. Held out season by season, 22-year-olds with 150+ PA cleared the bar 17% of the time, 24-year-olds 8%, anyone 26 or older 3 to 5%; a man with a full-time job clears the 350-PA half of the bar far more often than a part-timer. And young regulars with a modest rate jumped more often than young regulars already hitting (19% against 8%), because a breakout is measured off their own best and theirs is one step up instead of two. The model's number for young full-timers is calibrated: where it said 13%, 11% happened. The lookalikes are the check on any one man: if the twenty who looked most like him mostly stayed put, his number needs a better reason than his birthday, and the Statcast line is where to look for one.

Streamers: this week's matchups

Reading this page
  • Expected points for a hitter-day = his points per plate appearance × plate appearances per game, nudged for the opposing starter (quality and lefty/righty), the park, the weather at first pitch, home field and his last 30 days. Week pts adds up his games.
  • For a pitcher, a start is worth his points per start, nudged for how good the opposing lineup is against his hand, the park and the weather, plus a bonus when the opponent strikes out a lot. That lineup's bat is its season line, because a club's hot or cold month at the plate does not carry (one 25-game block predicts the next at r −0.01); its strikeout rate does carry (r 0.43), so a club whose Ks have moved lately gets a half-weight nudge and a chip saying so. 2 marks a two-start week. Ten starts a week is the league cap.
  • A grey P means the starter is projected from the rotation order and not yet posted by the club. Tick More stats to see each nudge as a multiplier.

Keepers and the 2027 draft pool

How the boards below work

Each team keeps 5 hitters and 2 pitchers; the draft only holds what is left. The board is ordered by multi-year value: 2027 points above replacement (what he beats a free agent by on the days he plays), plus the option to keep him again in 2028 and 2029 for whatever he is expected to beat the league's keeper line by, discounted 0.8 a year. Two players with the same 2027 projection are not worth the same slot if one is 24 and the other 34. Keep vs draft is that value minus what this team's own first-round pick is worth to them: positive means the slot beats drafting the best man left, and surplus means he clears that bar but someone else on the roster clears it by more. 2027 only is his 2027 points above replacement alone. The same keeper groups are used on My team, on the Trades page and to build the draft pool, so nothing on the site disagrees about who a team keeps. A two-way player counts as a pitcher keeper on any roster where his arm earns one of the two slots, and his bat is added to his pitcher value.

Rehab assignments

Reading this page
  • Straight from MLB's own transactions feed and minor-league box scores, not from anyone's write-up. A club files an assignment and it is here on the next refresh, with the outings as they happen.
  • Day 14 of 20 is the real clock: a rehab assignment is capped at 20 days for a position player and 30 for a pitcher, so the window is a harder deadline than any report. An arm past his window is usually on a second assignment or an agreed extension rather than about to be activated.
  • The outings are the tell. For a pitcher watch the pitch count climb, not the line: 1 inning on 15 pitches is a man several outings away, 5 innings on 75 is a man about to be activated. For a bat, watch at-bats per game reach a full game's worth.
  • Setback means he has not played in ten days or more. The clock stops when a man stops playing, and nobody files a transaction saying so.
  • An assignment closes itself: a rehab ends when the club activates, recalls, options or reassigns him, and the 40-man roster status is checked too, so a man who is off the injured list drops off this board whatever the last transaction said.

NFBC Postseason Holdem

One roster for the whole playoffs, scored in the contest's own points, with the multipliers that decide it. Bracket odds come from the exchanges; every player is projected in Holdem points for each round his club might play.

How the contest works, and how this page plays it
  • The format. Sixteen spots (C, 1B, 2B, 3B, SS, four OF, UT, six P), drafted once before the Division Series from the eight clubs still alive. At most three from any club and at least one from each, so the roster is 3-3-3-3-1-1-1-1. Between rounds you may replace only the men whose club was eliminated. Per club: two to six in the LCS, six to ten in the World Series.
  • The multipliers. A player held from the Division Series scores 1x there, 2x in the LCS and 3x in the World Series. One added for the LCS scores 1x then 2x. One added for the World Series scores 1x. The scoring: 1B 1, 2B 2, 3B 3, HR 4, R 1, RBI 1, SB 1, BB 1, HBP 1, each out (AB minus H) −0.25; IP 1, K 1, W 4, SV 4, ER −1.
  • Why it is a bracket contest. In each of the last four Octobers, 64 to 77 percent of a perfect entry's points came from the two World Series clubs, and about half from the six men held on them at triple points. The four Division Series losers were worth one to two percent between them. Pick the two pennant winners; everything else is detail.
  • What the page does. Bracket turns Kalshi and Polymarket title and pennant prices into a per-game strength for every club and plays the whole tournament out, so every series has a chance, an expected length and a chance of each game being played. Players projects each man in Holdem points per series (season rates shrunk to the league, lineup slot, playoff deflation, injured-list chances). Roster builds the best sixteen for any bracket you believe in, with the LCS and World Series replacements. Brackets ranks the likeliest paths for multi-entry. Playbook has the analyst reading and the last four Octobers in numbers.

How the numbers are made

Each score in plain words first, then the math behind it if you want it. Every check below was run on past seasons the model had not seen.

League points (verified against Fantrax)

Everything on this site is scored the way this league scores. Hits, homers, runs, RBI, walks and steals earn points for hitters; innings, strikeouts and quality starts earn points for pitchers, while earned runs and blown saves cost points.

Deserved points

What a hitter should have scored based on how hard and where he hit the ball, using Statcast's expected stats instead of his actual hits. Walks, hit-by-pitches and steals are skills, so they stay as they were. Luck = actual points − deserved points: a big positive number means the hits fell in and he is likely to come back down.

Show the math
xH = xBA × AB; xXBH = xISO × AB / (r2B + 2·r3B + 3·rHR); x1B = xH − xXBH x2B = xXBH·r2B; x3B = xXBH·r3B; xHR = xXBH·rHR (his own doubles/triples/homers mix, regressed to league over 40 XBH) xR/PA, xRBI/PA = ridge(xOBP, xISO, xBA, SB/PA, xHR/PA, sprint) deserved points = Σ weight(event) × x(event); luck = points − deserved points Check: across 987 consecutive 300-PA seasons, deserved points per PA predicted next year's points per PA with r = 0.52, actual points per PA only 0.47.

Points above replacement

How many points a player gives you over the free agent who would play instead, on the days he plays. A missed game is not a lost game here: the slot is filled from the bench or the wire at about replacement rate, so a hitter is worth what he beats a streamed bat by per game and a starter what he beats a streamed start by. Those rates were measured by replaying 2026 in this league's format (real rosters, daily lineups, the 10-start cap, all twelve teams streaming against each other): about 3.5 points a game for a hitter, a little less at catcher, and 7.2 a start. Every other position sits at the league-wide rate because the two utility slots and a deep wire even them out, so a utility-only hitter earns nothing extra.

Show the math
season (2026 table): points − r × games for a hitter (r = that season's 150th-best regular by points per game; catcher the lower of r and the 15th catcher), points − 7.2 × starts for a starter 2027 board: projected points − r_pos × projected games; projected starts × (projected points per start − 7.2) replay fit: hitters value ≈ points − 3.6 × games (R² .75, against .21 for points minus a fixed season total); starters points − 7.2 × starts (R² .85)

Beat his draft slot by

How many more (or fewer) points a hitter produced than the typical hitter drafted where he was drafted, using that year's preseason ADP. "Beat" means +75 points, 25 spots better than drafted, and a top-150 finish.

Show the math
expected(rank) = isotonic( rolling median of points by preseason ADP hitter rank ), per season beat slot by = points − expected(ADP rank); rank gain = ADP rank − final rank beat: ≥ +75 pts, ≥ +25 ranks, top-150 finish big beat: ≥ +150, ≥ +60, top-75

Aging curve

How much a hitter's points per plate appearance typically change from one age to the next, measured from every hitter in 2021–2026 with a full season both years. This league rewards singles and steals, which fade early, so the curve turns down harder and sooner than a home-run league's would. The projection and the trade analyzer both age players along this curve.

Projected 2027 points

A player's expected points next year: his rate times the plate appearances he can be expected to get. The rate is one regression on this year's Statcast skills, deserved points, discipline, speed and age, with his own last two seasons sitting alongside them as inputs, so the model decides for itself how much of a man's past to believe (it believes last year a great deal and two years ago hardly at all). It replaced a boosted tree in September 2026 because with 1,300 seasons to learn from the tree was memorising them: held out a season at a time the regression is closer in every one of five years. Playing time is its own fitted model. It starts from his pace while he was actually on the roster (a late call-up or an injury-shortened year does not read as a part-time player; pace is now measured the same way for every training season, 2021 on), trimmed for injury history, then reads his age and two market views: where he was drafted last March and where he actually finished. Before the finish rank was in, last March's opinion held breakouts back (top-50 finishers the market had outside its top 75 got 63 more plate appearances the next year than projected) and propped up stars who had a bad year. Age only counts past 30 and scales with how much he plays, because one straight-line age term cut healthy veteran regulars too hard (33+ with 600+ PA got 572 the next year against 508 projected). And the model now learns from the seasons that never happened: a hitter who lost the next year to injury counts as the few plate appearances he got, not as a missing row, so the projection is an honest expectation with injury risk in it. Sourced news the model cannot see (a surgery that costs half of 2027) is applied on top and labelled on the card.

Show the math
rate = ridge(Statcast skills, deserved points, discipline, speed, age, this year's pts/PA and PA, last year's pts/PA and PA, two years ago pts/PA and PA) ridge alpha 30, standardised inputs, medians for a missing prior season; flat from alpha 3 to 100 out of sample, so not a tuned number no aging step is added afterwards: the model's own age term carries it, and adding half a step back moved r by .001 and made the error worse pace = PA per 162 team games while on the active roster (IL stints and days before the call-up excluded), every season 2021-26 base PA = (0.6 × this year's pace + 0.4 × mean pace of the previous two seasons with 150+ PA) × (1 − 0.0008 × weighted IL days) × (0.95 if 33+) projected PA = linear fit of next-year PA on base PA, PA, (age − 30)+, (26 − age)+, base PA × (age − 30)+, PA × (age − 30)+, log ADP rank, log final rank, its square, log ADP × log final rank; trained on every hitter-season with 150+ PA, a lost next season counted as the PA the MLB feed shows (0 if none), except 35+ (treated as retired); clipped 200-700 projected points = rate × projected PA Check (leave-one-season-out, 200+ PA both years): Spearman .500 against .451 for the boosted tree it replaced, mean error .087 against .091, and it wins every held-out season including 2025→26 (.446 against .378), the year every method finds hardest. Breakout %

The model's chance that next season is a new career level for a hitter: points per plate appearance at least 0.08 above his previous best, with 350+ plate appearances and a top-90 finish. It looks at his Statcast skills, the gap between deserved and actual points, how far he sits below his own best, his age, playing time, and how his barrels and bat speed changed this year. Everything is read as a percentile within its own season, so a number from 2021 and one from 2026 mean the same thing. Three models vote (a boosted tree and two regressions, one on all the inputs and one on the eight that matter most) and the average is calibrated against every held-out season, so 20% means one in five. A veteran who already had his best years cannot score high, by design.

The evidence under each number. The Why line is the eight-input regression's own arithmetic: each input's coefficient times how far he sits from the average hitter, the three biggest terms put into words with his real numbers, plus a note when his barrel rate or bat speed moved on last year. Age lines carry the empirical rate, how often hitters that age with 150+ PA actually jumped in the training seasons (22: 17%, 24: 8%, 26 and older: 3 to 5%). The Statcast chips are his numbers with where each sits among this season's hitters at 100+ PA; green past the 75th percentile, red under the 25th, with strikeouts and chase rate read the low-is-good way. The lookalikes are the twenty player-seasons nearest him in the model's own training pairs on age, playing time, rate, contact quality, strikeouts, speed, career length, luck and this year's changes, never his own earlier seasons, with what each did the next year. On its own the lookalike rate scores .70 AUC held out against the model's .81, so it is the check on a number, not a second forecast: when a high number comes with lookalikes who mostly stayed put, the reasons and the chips are where to decide whether to believe it.

Show the math
breakout(t+1) := pts/PA(t+1) ≥ career-best(200+ PA seasons through t) + 0.08 AND PA(t+1) ≥ 350 AND final rank(t+1) ≤ 90 Breakout % = 100 × P(breakout | skills, deserved-points gap, career best, gap to best, seasons, age, PA, yoy barrel and bat-speed change), inputs as within-season percentiles P = average on the logit scale of three models trained on 2021→22 … 2025→26: a boosted tree (26 inputs), a logistic regression on the same 26, and a logistic regression on eight (age, PA, gap to best, luck, sprint speed, exit velocity, career PA, K% − BB%) then Platt-calibrated on the leave-one-season-out predictions and capped at the highest probability any held-out player ever earned (about 52%) held out a season at a time: AUC .81 pooled against .77 for the tree alone; the 40 highest scores each year break out 17.5% of the time against 14.5%, on a base rate under 5%

Breakout %, leave-one-season-out

AUC is how well the model separates breakouts from the rest (0.5 = coin flip, 1 = perfect). Top-40 hit = share of its 40 highest scores who really broke out, against the base rate for everyone.

Points projection, leave-one-season-out

r = correlation with what actually happened the next year; higher is better. "Carry-over" is the naive forecast that next year equals this year.

Using 2025 data only, the 25 highest Breakout % hitters produced a new-level 2026 at a 16% clip against a 3.3% base rate; Caglianone, Otto Lopez, Crow-Armstrong and Meidroth were all inside that top 25. Trout's 2027 Breakout % is 1.9.

Multi-year value and keep vs draft

Two numbers, always under these two names. Multi-year value is 2027 points above replacement plus the option to keep him again in 2028 and 2029 (only what he is expected to beat the league's keeper line by counts, discounted 0.8 a year); it decides the keeper order. Keep vs draft is that value minus what his team's own first-round pick is worth to them: how much a keeper slot beats simply drafting the best man left. The verdict reads it against that pick: lock when he is worth more than the pick, clear keep at 30% of it, marginal above zero. A player outside his team's best five hitters and two pitchers is surplus (worth more to someone else, so trade him) or let go.

Show the math
multi-year value = PAR_2027 + 0.8 × E[max(0, PAR_2028 − line)] + 0.64 × E[max(0, PAR_2029 − line)] line = PAR of the 60th hitter / 24th starter (a two-way player kept in a pitcher slot is left out of the hitter line) keep vs draft = multi-year value − value of that team's own round-1 pick to that team verdict: kept and ≥ pick → lock; ≥ 0.3 × pick → clear keep; > 0 → marginal; outside the 5 + 2 → surplus if > 0, else let go

Trade value and draft picks

A trade is scored by rebuilding each team's best keeper group (5 hitters + 2 pitchers) before and after. A player's worth to a team is what he adds to that group in keeper value: 2027 points above replacement (projected points minus about 3.5 a game for a hitter, 7.2 a start for a pitcher, rates measured by replaying 2026 in this league's format), plus the option to keep him again in 2028 and 2029 for what he is expected to beat the league's keeper line by, discounted 0.8 a year and aged along the curves above. A draft pick is worth half of what the 2027 board says is left at that pick and half of what picks at that slot have actually returned in this league since 2024 (a bust counts as zero: you drop him and stream the spot).

Show the math

Prospect score

A 0–100 grade for how close a minor-league hitter is to helping: pedigree (MLB Pipeline rank) is 35 of the points, being young for his level is 25, his minor-league bat (power, on-base, walks vs strikeouts, steals, compared with hitters at the same level) is 25, and the quality of any big-league sample (contact quality, bat speed) is 15.

Show the math
prospect score = 35·pedigree + 25·level_age + 25·milb_bat + 15·mlb_sample pedigree = 1 − (Pipeline rank − 1)/100 (0.15 unranked); level_age = clip(0.5 + (avg age at level − age)·0.15, 0, 1) × level weight (AAA 1, AA .85, A+ .6, A .4) milb_bat = 0.5 + 0.2·z( ISO, OBP, BB−K, ½·SB/600 ) within level; mlb_sample = mean percentile of xwOBA, EV50, bat speed

Each minor-league line on a prospect card is colored by its percentile against every hitter with 100+ PA at the same level in the same season. The Statcast block comes from Savant's minor-league search (Triple-A parks carry Hawk-Eye): exit velocity, barrels, whiffs and chases, with percentiles inside that season's prospect cohort.

Pitching: Stuff+, Location+, Pitching+

Three grades on a 100-is-average scale, built the way FanGraphs' and PitcherList's versions are framed. Stuff+ grades the pitches themselves (whiffs, in-zone misses, put-away rate, velocity, how unusual the movement is). Location+ grades command (edges, first strikes, chases, walks, meatballs). Pitching+ is the blend that best predicts next year's points per start. The real models use pitch-by-pitch tracking that is not public; these are season-level stand-ins from Savant's leaderboards, and the check below is what they are worth in this league's scoring.

Show the math
Stuff+ = 100 + 10·z[ z(whiff%) + z(in-zone swing-miss%) + z(arsenal put-away%) + z(fastball velo) + ½·z(movement uniqueness) + z(arsenal RV/100 vs hitters) ] / 5.5 Location+ = 100 + 10·z[ z(edge%) + z(first-strike%) + ½·z(zone%) − z(meatball%) − z(BB%) + z(chase%) ] / 5.5 Pitching+ = 100 + 10·z[ ridge(Stuff+, Location+, xERA, K−BB%) → next-year points per start ] arsenal: pitch types with 10+% usage; put-away = K / 2-strike pitches; z-scores among starters with 50+ IP in the same season

Streamers

A hitter-day is worth his points per plate appearance × his usual plate appearances, nudged for the opposing starter (his quality this year, plus the hitter's lefty/righty split), the park (Savant's three-year run factors by batter side), the weather at first pitch (temperature and the wind blowing out to center, from the park's orientation), home field and his last 30 days. A pitcher's start is worth his points per start (starts only, from game logs), nudged for the opposing lineup's quality and strikeouts against his hand, the park and the weather. Rotations are projected from each club's recent starters until the probable is posted; the website re-reads the posted probables, lineups, scores and forecasts every ten minutes.

Show the math
hitter exp pts = base pts/PA × PA/G × f_SP^0.6 × f_platoon × f_park × f_weather × f_home × f_form f_SP = clip(SP xwOBA / league xwOBA, 0.75, 1.3), regressed to league over 300 batters faced; f_platoon from his wOBA vs L / vs R (regressed); f_park = Savant runs factor by batter side / 100 f_weather = clip(1 + 0.003 × (temp − 70), 0.94, 1.06) × clip(1 + 0.004 × wind-out mph, 0.92, 1.08), 1.0 under a roof; f_form from 30-day wOBA (regressed) pitcher exp pts = base pts/GS × f_opp × f_park^0.5 × f_weather^0.5 × f_home + K bonus from the opponent's K% vs that hand f_opp = 1 − 3.5·(opponent wOBA vs his hand − .312), capped at ±30%; K bonus = 0.85 · his K per start · (opponent K% / .222 − 1) those two strengths were checked against the same 3,072 starts: a .020 swing in the opponent's wOBA is worth 0.71 points, three points of opponent K% 0.56, and home 0.60 (most of which the park factor already carries) base pts/GS = 0.27 + 0.110·Pitching+ + 1.203·IP-mix + 0.039·ΔCSW + 0.808·Δvelo − 26.70·xwOBA against IP-mix = half his innings per start this season, half his last three starts (shrunk toward 5.2 IP on a thin sample) ΔCSW, Δvelo = called-strikes-plus-whiffs per pitch, and fastball velocity, over the last 30 days against his own season — both measured over the games he STARTED, because a reliever's one max-effort inning is not his starting velocity. Filtering the trend to starts is worth a lot: velocity carries 0.84 points a start per mph that way, against 0.15 when relief outings are mixed in. fitted on 3,072 real 2026 starts, each predicted from what was known before it, five-fold by pitcher a starter with no Savant line falls back to his own starts-only rate shrunk toward what his Pitching+ implies Which of the metrics analysts name actually predict THIS league's points, one at a time, against the next start: Pitching+ .332 xwOBA against .327 Stuff+ .320 strikeouts per start .291 his own points per start .294 K−BB% .260 K% .247 IP per start .229 SwStr% .227 CSW% .212 whiff% .205 ERA .197 BB% .105 Two of those readings are specific to this league. Strikeouts per START beat K% (.291 vs .247), because a strikeout is worth a point here rather than propping up a rate. And BB% barely registers, because there is no walk penalty at all — a walk only costs through the baserunner and the shorter outing. K−BB% is the analysts' simple workhorse and it does beat K% here, but contact quality beats both: xwOBA against earned a place in the formula, while strikeouts per start, quality-start rate and K−BB% all took a zero weight once Pitching+ and innings were already in. End to end, against the same 3,072 starts with the matchup multipliers applied: r 0.380, MAE 4.49, against r 0.375 and 4.50 for the version before contact quality went in, and r 0.315 / 4.67 for the original season-rate blend. Two things were tried and left out because they made it worse. Recent RESULTS: predicting the next start from the last three starts alone scores r 0.241, worse than the season-to-date rate's 0.288, and a regression given both puts almost no weight on the recent one. Starts-only SKILL LEVELS: rebuilding the level term from his CSW, K−BB% and velocity over his starts alone scores r 0.303 against 0.364 for season-long Pitching+ across every appearance, and when both are offered the fit drops the starts-only version entirely. Relief innings add sample to a pitcher's stuff rather than noise — it is the trend, not the level, that has to be filtered to starts. A pitcher whose last outing came out of the bullpen, or who has not started in twelve days, is left out of the projected rotations entirely — a posted probable still overrides it. Where a game has no posted probable the rotation is cycled forward from who actually started recently, with two rules that decide whether anyone gets a second start in the week: each club keeps at least five arms in the cycle (a shorter one manufactures second starts for whoever is in front) an arm is only projected once he has had HIS OWN turn — the median gap between his starts this season, floored at five days and capped at seven. Across 2026 starts the gap was six days half the time, five days a third of the time and four days or fewer 0.4% of the time, so a man is never projected on four days' rest and the arms a club gives extra rest are not squeezed in early. if no arm on the roster has had his turn, the game is left TBD rather than guessed. Hitters facing a TBD starter get a neutral opposing-pitcher factor, and the live layer fills the name in as soon as MLB posts it.
