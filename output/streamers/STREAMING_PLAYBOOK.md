# Streaming playbook: Scott White (CBS) and Nick Pollock (PitcherList), and how the lab's Streamers tab uses them

Reference for the Philly Special project. Built from the columns read on 2026-09-13 (Week 26 sleeper hitters, sleeper pitchers and two-start rankings; a 2024 Week 24 sleeper-hitters column; PitcherList's The List 8/25 and the 9/8 playoff "expected schedules" edition; the PitcherList terms glossary). This is a sample of the columns, not every article from the last few years; the recurring structure and vocabulary below is what repeats week to week. Quotes are kept under 15 words.

## 1. Scott White's weekly columns (CBS Sports)

Three columns every weekend for the coming Fantasy week (Mon–Sun), each "updated Sunday evening":

**Sleeper hitters (Top 10).** Ten hitters "rostered in less than 80 percent of CBS Sports leagues" who are "not must-starts by any estimation" but the best off the wire. Each entry shows the week's matchups (e.g. "@COL4, SEA3"), roster percentage, and a two-to-four sentence case. The column ends with "Best hitter matchups" and "Worst hitter matchups" lists, five teams each, ranked by the pitching staffs they face.

What he weighs, in the order it shows up in the text:
- Schedule volume and venue: a four-game series, a full week at home, "a full week" at Coors. Rockies hitters at home and visitors to Coors are automatic candidates; he cites a player's home/road OPS gap (Carrigg's "200 points higher at home").
- Handedness math: he counts how many lefties or righties a team is scheduled to face ("three lefties in six games") and pairs it with the hitter's platoon split (Mayo, Encarnacion-Strand vs LHP; Melendez, Tena vs RHP). Strict platoon bats only get recommended when the schedule lines up; "mashable righties" is his phrase for the soft side.
- Hot hand: last 4 to 26 games, quoted as "11 for 18" or ".341 (30 for 88)". He will recommend a hot hitter with bad matchups (Butler, Crow-Armstrong in 2024) and says so explicitly.
- Playing time confirmation: "started all nine games", "back from the IL and started three straight", batting leadoff with a star out, and the lineup-spot caveat ("typically bats eighth").
- Skills evidence when it exists: exit velocities "to back it up", walks nearly matching strikeouts, strikeout rate as the volatility warning (Mayo's 31 percent).
- Catchers are a reluctant recommendation ("nobody is looking to use one in a utility spot") unless they are playing every day.

**Sleeper pitchers (Top 10).** "Streaming pitchers isn't for the faint of heart"; ten arms rostered in no more than 80 percent, and "only the top two or three are full-throated endorsements, while the rest are more about making the best of a bad situation." Evidence he uses: the opponent's offense rank ("fourth-worst offense", "22nd overall"), a recent stretch line over 6–10 starts (ERA, WHIP, K/9), velocity ticks ("average fastball velocity is the highest since 2022", "an extra tick"), swinging-strike rate, a pitch-mix change (leaning on a cutter), and, for points leagues specifically, volume: two-start weeks and RP eligibility "come in particularly handy" where "volume is paramount". He separates ratio risk from strikeout upside and tells you which he is chasing.

**Two-start pitcher rankings.** Every two-start arm for the week, "subject to change" because it requires forecasting rotations, sorted into four buckets: "Must-start, all formats", "Advisable in most cases", "Better left for points leagues", "No thanks". The third bucket is the one that matters for Philly Special: pitchers whose two turns are worth the innings and strikeouts in a points format even when the ratios are shaky.

Vocabulary that recurs: sleeper (under 80 percent rostered, not a must-start); hot-hand play; injury fill-in; must-add; speculative add; mashable; favorable slate; matchups (always quoted as team-count strings); "you know the drill" for a platoon bat he has recommended before; "check back Sunday" (the lists are revised Sunday night when probables firm up).

Hit-rate note: his Week 24 (2024) list had Pete Crow-Armstrong at 44 percent rostered as a hot-hand play; PCA finished 2026 as the number one hitter in this league's scoring. Lawrence Butler led that same list.

## 2. Nick Pollock's PitcherList

**The List.** Top 100 starting pitchers, updated every Monday, written for "5x5, 12-teamer, H2H format focused (Wins, not QS)", so adjust for a QS-and-innings points league: his Tobys and Hollys are worth more here than in his format, and his win-dependent notes matter less. Rules he states: IL arms and pitchers not confirmed in a rotation are off The List (an injury table gives their rank "when healthy"); the back half of the list is the waiver wire and moves aggressively; two labels max per pitcher. Tiers are named ("It's Nice To Not Worry", "The Other Aces", "You're Dope But Not Dope. Yet.", "Far Sweeter Than Sour", "What Will They Become?", "It's Something. It's A Toby.", "Arms To Consider This Week"). He points readers to the daily SP Roundup for the reasoning behind every move.

**The playoff editions (8/31 and 9/8).** For the last month of the season The List becomes team-by-team expected rotation tables: every starter's next three or four starts with the date and opponent, "(OFF)" marking a start after an off day, AVO (Average Value of Opponent) as a strength-of-schedule number, and a tier per pitcher: Auto, Solid, Tobys & Bombs, Early/This Week Streamer, Later Streamer, Desperate Streamer, Do Not Start. Opponent quality comes from PitcherList's PLV projections team-offense table, split vL and vR, in five buckets (Top / Solid / Average / Weak / Poor). He warns that "these schedules are going to change" and that the daily streaming article is the live version.

**Terms you will see constantly (from the PitcherList glossary):**
- AGA (Aces Gonna Ace), Coronation Day (the start that earns the AGA tag), TATIAGA.
- Holly (a Toby with 20–25% strikeouts and good ratios; Messick is "the greatest Holly of them all"), Toby (steady floor, little upside, avoid against tough lineups), Spider-Man/Tobey Maguire (the best Toby right now), Vargas Rule (a historically mediocre pitcher performing; keep starting him until he gives a reason not to), Baha Man (career-year one-hit wonder).
- Cherry Bomb (great start or a dud, no telling which), HIPSTER (headache pitcher with innings or consistency questions), PEAS (Poor Execution, Awesome Stuff), Frizzle (an upside dart you take for a week), Young Gun, Shag Rug ("a young man's floor"), PILOT Rule (Prospect's Initial Line Often Terrible: do not start a debut), Still ILL (first start back from the IL), Frozen Banana, Werewolf, Dennis, Double Bubble, Panda (waiver-wire fodder), Frank, Cup of Schmo.
- Arsenal language: BSB (Blake Snell Blueprint: fastballs up, everything else down), Ras Pack (cutter + sinker + four-seam), SWATCH (southpaw with a tight changeup), Money Pitch (40%+ chase, 40%+ zone, 15%+ swinging strike), Dancing With The Disco (slider-only), Huascar Rule (do not trust two-pitch arms unless both are elite or the best one is a four-seamer), Empty Velocity, Canibal McSanchez (cutters at the top), Free Real Estate, Neckbeard, Free-Strike/NC rate, iVB and HAVAA for fastball shape, CSW.
- Matchup language: Reds Carpet (Reds on the road are a cushy matchup), Rockie Road (stream against the Rockies on the road), Rocky Mountain Way (Rockies pitchers only on the road), Sacré Verde (the Athletics' Sacramento park, hitter-friendly), The Green Mile (Sacramento then Coors), @TEA (Seattle, "the anti-Coors"), Natty Light, NL Easy, Blame it on the [Padres/Reds/Marlins], DANG (day game after night game; catchers sit).
- Outcome language: Gallows Pole and King Cole (most whiffs, highest CSW on the night), Golden Goal (both), Gold Star, Singled Out (BABIP blowup rather than homers), Careful Icarus (left in too long), One Night Bland, BITTEN, Philly (5.2 IP and out), PQS/VPQS (a quality start that still hurt), Bailey Special, Dusty Donut, Wonka, HAISTBMBWT (one strikeout or none), Koufax (the luck god), HOTEL (BABIP + LOB + HR/FB), Dodgeritis, Cardinal Sin.

## 3. How the lab's Streamers tab maps to those ideas

| Heuristic in the columns | What the tab does |
|---|---|
| "Rockies at home all week", Coors visitors | Savant 3-year run park factor by batter side (Coors 125, T-Mobile 85, Globe Life 88) multiplies every game |
| Count of lefties/righties faced, platoon bats | Projected opposing starter's hand for each game plus the hitter's own vL/vR wOBA (regressed) |
| "Mashable" opposing pitcher, opponent offense rank | Opposing SP's regressed xwOBA allowed (and his L/R split) raised to the 0.6 power for hitters; opponent team wOBA and K% vs the pitcher's hand for starters |
| Hot hand | Last-30-day wOBA vs season wOBA, half weight |
| Playing time | PA per game from the last 30 days; IL, minors and off-40-man players hidden; catcher DANG not modeled |
| Two-start weeks, "volume is paramount" | Two-start flag, weekly expected points, 10-start cap check against your own rotation |
| Weather (not in either column) | Open-Meteo temperature and wind component toward center field at first pitch; roofed parks ignored |
| "Subject to change" rotations | Posted probables used where they exist; the rest are projected from each club's recent rotation order and tagged P |

Weights are in `breakout/streamers.py` and are deliberately mild (each factor moves a game by a few percent; Coors and a bad starter are the only things that move it 20–40 percent). Rerun `python -m breakout.pipeline_streamers` any morning to refresh the week with the latest probables, rosters and forecasts.

Scott White's ten sleeper hitters: Ty France, Cole Carrigg, TJ Rumfield, Griffin Conine, Jakob Marsee, Leonardo Bernal, Tommy Edman, Christian Encarnacion-Strand, Coby Mayo, Kyle Karros. Best hitter matchups by his ranking: Tigers, Padres, Rockies, Dodgers, Guardians.

The lab's top free agents for the same week (as of Sunday): TJ Rumfield 38.7, Troy Johnston 31.4, Connor Norby 30.9, Tommy Edman 30.8, Xander Bogaerts 30.4, Kyle Karros 29.8, Luis Campusano 28.6 — the same Coors and Dodgers-vs-Reds logic, arrived at from park factors, splits and projected starters. Two of his ten (France, Carrigg) are already on Bort's roster; France is on the paternity list as of Sunday. His two-start must-starts (Skubal, Misiorowski, Yamamoto, Sánchez, Detmers, Wacha) overlap with the lab's projected two-start list only partly, because both sides are guessing at rotations on a Sunday; the lab shows a P tag on every projected start for that reason.
