Demographics
Teams keep growing prospects up the middle. A year ago, a record was set with 30 shortstops on the re-ranked Top 100. That mark didn’t last long. After placing 28 shortstops on the preseason list, we have 33 filling up the rankings now who primarily play the premium position. Our top four, and eight of our top 16, are typically found at short.
Outfielders come next with 27, while pitchers aren’t far behind at 25. That contingent is split 16-9 righties vs. lefties. We have eight catchers, four third basemen, two first baseman and one at second base to round out the list.
It tends to be a Draft-heavy list, and this edition is no different. A total of 68 players came courtesy of the Draft, with the remaining 32 coming via the international market. There are nine countries represented, led by the United States, with 69. The Dominican Republic (12) and Venezuela (10) are next while Canada (3) and Cuba (2) are the other two nations with multiple representatives. The Bahamas, Colombia, Mexico and Spain each have one prospect that was born on its soil.
A dozen players on the list have already changed hands via trade, led by Leo De Vries at No. 2. Zyhir Hope has been dealt twice, and is one of six on this edition of the Top 100 who was traded at this year's Deadline, along with Arjun Nimmala, Anthony Eyanson, River Ryan, Jefferson Rojas and Ramon Marquez.
Farm reports
Our updated farm system rankings are coming soon, but we can take a quick snapshot of which teams have the most high-end talent. There are three organizations with six Top 100 guys each: the Dodgers, Marlins and Mariners. Four teams have five on the list apiece: the Brewers, Giants, Rays and Tigers.
One of the many ways we try to measure the strength of a system is what we call Prospect Points: 100 for the No. 1 prospect, 99 for No. 2 and on down to one point for No. 100. Not surprisingly, the Dodgers lead the way in this category with 417 PP and the Mariners are second (369). The Rays (284), Giants (283) and Tigers (281) are the only other teams over 280 points.
